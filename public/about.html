The Case

Google mapped the internet.
The informal economy doesn't live there.

In South Africa, the feature phone never went away. Roughly five million are sold every year, and millions more are kept as a second phone: the one you take when you don't want to risk the good one. They are not on Maps, not on Yelp, not on Search. The default assumption that a smartphone is the entry ticket to information quietly excludes the parallel device economy that already runs on these phones.

Source is the routing layer for the people the internet skipped. A single SMS reaches Claude Opus 4.7, which compresses any answer (clinic hours, cement prices, kombi schedules) into 160 characters and sends it back. No app. No data. No onboarding.

The same backend serves both browser simulations and live SMS through a Twilio webhook. There is no mock layer between the demo and the real thing. What you text from your laptop is what Gogo texts from her Nokia.

The Mechanism
Channel
Twilio SMS webhook. Inbound messages arrive at POST /sms as application/x-www-form-urlencoded, exactly as a production telco delivery would.
Engine
Claude Opus 4.7 with adaptive extended thinking. The model reasons internally about constraint, context, and compression before producing a 160-character output. No post-processing truncation.
Memory
Supabase ledger persists supply entries and home-node geo tags. On boot, the server rehydrates inventory from the last 24 hours so a container restart never loses the market.
Languages
All eleven official South African languages, plus township slang and natural code-switching. The model responds in whatever language the user texts in.
Decay
Stock entries expire after 24 hours. Fresh entries (under six hours) are prioritized; older entries are flagged for verification. The market cleans itself.
